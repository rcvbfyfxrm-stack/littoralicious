Umami in Western Cuisine: Fermentation as the Universal Glutamate Engine

Mouritsen and Ono catalogue Western condiments that deliver umami through fermentation and aging — and propose “umamification” as a deliberate strategy for plant-forward cuisine. The key finding: fermentation is the universal mechanism for generating free glutamate, and Western food traditions have been doing it for centuries.

The Paper

Ono, M. & Mouritsen, O.G., International Journal of Gastronomy and Food Science, 2025. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijgfs.2025.101305 (peer-reviewed; no industry funding declared).

Mouritsen (University of Copenhagen; co-author of Umami: Unlocking the Secrets of the Fifth Taste, Columbia University Press, 2014) catalogues Western condiments that deliver umami through fermentation and ageing, and proposes “umamification” as a deliberate strategy for plant-forward cuisine.

Umami was identified as the fifth taste by Kikunae Ikeda at Tokyo Imperial University (Journal of the Chemical Society of Tokyo, 1909), who isolated monosodium glutamate from kombu. The dedicated taste receptor T1R1/T1R3 was characterised almost a century later by Nelson et al. (Nature, 416, 2002) — confirming that free L-glutamate has its own perceptual channel, distinct from sweet, sour, salty, and bitter. Synergy with 5′-ribonucleotides (IMP, GMP) can amplify glutamate response by up to 8× (Yamaguchi, Journal of Food Science, 32, 1967), which is the mechanism behind dashi, anchovy-on-tomato, and parmesan-on-meat-stock.

Free Glutamate in Western Sources

Western Source Free Glutamate (mg/100g) Mechanism
Marmite/Vegemite 1,960 Yeast autolysis
Parmigiano-Reggiano (24-month) 1,200–1,680 Proteolysis during aging
Roquefort 1,280 Fungal proteolysis
Worcestershire sauce 830 Fish fermentation + malt vinegar aging
Anchovy paste 630 Halophilic bacterial fermentation
Tomato paste (concentrated) 580 Enzymatic concentration

Galley Implications

Deep umami in a vegetable-forward menu is achievable from the Western pantry alone. Parmesan rind in the stock (proteolytic glutamate), anchovy paste folded into the base (halophilic-bacterial glutamate plus IMP from fish flesh), Worcestershire as a finishing agent. With Yamaguchi’s 1967 synergy in mind, the IMP from anchovy and the free glutamate from parmesan multiply rather than add. For yacht chefs provisioning in remote ports without miso or kombu, this is the practical replacement.

What This Does Not Mean

Asian and Western umami sources are not interchangeable on the plate. Free glutamate is the shared backbone; the volatile superstructure (Maillard pyrazines in soy sauce, lactones in aged parmesan, sulfides in anchovy) is what reads as Japanese, Italian, or English. Stack across traditions; do not substitute one for another and expect the same flavour signature.

Limitations

Review paper synthesising existing literature; not new analytical data. Glutamate values vary substantially by brand, batch, and age — a 12-month parmesan and a 36-month parmesan are not the same ingredient. Full text behind paywall.
